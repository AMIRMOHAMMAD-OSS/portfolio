Under review Phaseek

A hybrid model for multiscale prediction and generation of phase separating protein regions

Amir M. MohammadHosseini, Hossein Teimouri, Vincent Gureghian, Rayan Najjar, Ariel B. Lindner, Amir Pandi

bioRxiv · doi:10.1101/2025.01.27.635039

Pandi Lab · Sorbonne Université, CNRS, ERL U1338 Inserm (CQSB), Paris

Phaseek is a hybrid model integrating contextual sequence encoding with statistical graph representations to score the liquid–liquid phase separation (LLPS) propensity of amino acid sequences. It can identify phase-separating proteins and the key regions driving phase separation, as well as the effects of point mutations on phase separation. Phaseek is also used to design de novo short-length phase-separating peptide tags (25–80 aa long), which, fused with several proteins (GFP, RFdiffusion-generated binders, nanobodies, …), showed successful phase separation.

In progress NeurALPS

NeurALPS — Neural network for assembling lines of non-ribosomal peptide synthetases

Under the supervision of Dr. Amir Pandi, we are developing NeurALPS, a self-supervised learning framework for analyzing engineered nonribosomal peptide synthetase (NRPS) assembly lines. It combines frozen ESM-C protein embeddings with a contextual transformer pretrained on natural assemblies through masked reconstruction and contrastive ranking. We evaluate the learned representations for activity prediction using donor-held-out validation against engineered NRPS assembly lines from the Bode Lab, and we build exploratory domain- and boundary-level compatibility maps to assess contextual support for engineered fragment substitutions, with reproducible training and evaluation workflows.

Pandi Lab (Sorbonne Université, CNRS, ERL U1338 Inserm, CQSB, Paris) · in collaboration with the Bode Lab (Max Planck Institute for Terrestrial Microbiology, Marburg)

In progress CodonTransformer2

CodonTransformer2

After the success of CodonTransformer, we are now developing CodonTransformer2, a species-conditioned protein-to-codon language model that learns and generates biologically optimized coding sequences. CT2 scales to 27,000 species of coverage, trains on a substantially larger dataset, and can be fine-tuned on new species beyond those seen during training. It integrates host-species embeddings with codon-aware representations to predict synonymous codon usage and enable controlled, host-specific sequence generation for protein engineering and de novo sequence optimization.

Pandi Lab · Sorbonne Université, CNRS, ERL U1338 Inserm (CQSB), Paris

Other projects & coursework

Competition

EGFR binders — Adaptyv Bio Round 2 protein design competition

I designed 20 binders targeting Epidermal Growth Factor (EGF), of which 6 passed my in-silico filters and advanced to wet-lab evaluation. Starting from the EGFR–TGF-α co-crystal structure, I fixed the interface anchor residues, generated backbones with RFdiffusion under those constraints, and designed sequences with ProteinMPNN. I re-scored the final models with structure and interface filters and validated the top designs by BLI (kon, koff, KD).

Course project

Xylanase GH10 classifier from rumen metagenomes

I identified xylanase genes in ruminant rumen metagenome contigs. I filtered candidate sequences with BLAST+, HMMER and PSI-BLAST (PSSM), clustered them with CD-HIT, and translated them to protein. I then built a classifier to separate GH10 xylanases from neighbouring glycoside hydrolase families (GH8, GH9, GH11, GH12) using an encoder-only Transformer alongside classical ML, with Markov-chain oversampling to balance the classes.

With Pedram Daliri and Ali Asadi · supervised by Prof. Kaveh Kavoosi

Single-cell RNA-seq signatures of healthy blood

Omics course project analysing a 10X Genomics healthy blood sample (7,551 cells). The Scanpy pipeline covers quality control (UMI, gene and mitochondrial-content filters), normalization, highly variable gene selection and scaling, PCA, and clustering with three algorithms, followed by marker-gene interpretation of the recovered cell populations.
