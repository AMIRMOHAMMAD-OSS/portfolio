Selected research

Phaseek

We combined a Transformer with physicochemical graph features to predict liquid–liquid phase separation from protein sequence and identify the residues that drive it. We then used the model to design peptide tags. When fused to superfolder GFP, 70% of the tested tags induced phase separation in vivo.

A. M. MohammadHosseini, H. Teimouri, A. M. Cheraghali, et al. “A hybrid model for multiscale prediction and generation of phase separating protein regions.” bioRxiv, 2025.

Licensed by Inserm Transfert for non-commercial research use. European patent application EP26300824.5 covers phase-separating peptide generation.

NeurALPS

We trained NeurALPS on natural nonribosomal peptide synthetases to score engineered assembly lines and individual domains. The model reconstructs masked domain and linker embeddings using local self-attention and gated cross-attention to learned global tokens. We evaluated its scores against experimental peptide detection in three datasets of engineered constructs and are testing new designs with Helge Bode’s group.

CodonTransformer2

We curated approximately 150 million coding sequences from 26,678 organisms and trained a species-conditioned ModernBERT to predict synonymous codon usage. Amino-acid-specific mask tokens preserve the protein sequence, while learned host embeddings condition codon prediction on the target organism.

Other projects and coursework

EGFR binder design

For the second round of the Adaptyv Bio competition, I used the EGFR–TGF-α interface to guide backbone generation with RFdiffusion and sequence design with ProteinMPNN. I screened 20 designs and selected six for experimental evaluation.

GH10 xylanases in rumen metagenomes

I screened rumen metagenomes with BLAST and profile HMMs, then trained a classifier to distinguish GH10 xylanases from four related glycoside hydrolase families. With Pedram Daliri and Ali Asadi, supervised by Kaveh Kavoosi.

Single-cell RNA sequencing of healthy blood

I analyzed 7,551 cells from a 10x Genomics sample in Scanpy, compared three clustering algorithms, and used marker genes to identify blood cell populations. Supervised by Mahya Mehrmohammadi.

Molecular dynamics with GROMACS

In Azadeh Kordzadeh’s molecular dynamics course, I simulated proteins in explicit solvent, lipid membranes, and small molecule–nanomaterial systems. The exercises included steered molecular dynamics and umbrella sampling.
